0. OBJECTIVE, AUTHORITY AND USER INTENT

The user wants background intelligence that produces artifacts for different
stakeholders at different times: ordinary updates plus critical information that
can trigger escalation, including agent-detected situations with some defined
conditions. This document specifies a proposed bounded interpretation of that
capability. The user has not selected recipients, numeric thresholds, time zones,
response SLAs, delivery channels, first prediction target or autonomous-action scope.

This task only documents and discusses. No messages are sent, accounts connected,
models run, dashboards implemented or infrastructure deployed. Proposed examples
below use fabricated values and policies; they are not Olist findings or approved
business rules. A local demo should use an in-app inbox/simulated delivery log,
not real Slack/Teams/email messages or calls to sellers/customers.

PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
Recompute when relevant evidence changes; publish when results are accepted;
interrupt a person only when an approved policy says attention is warranted.
Artifact refresh frequency is not notification frequency.

1. WHAT IS AN ARTIFACT?

A durable, versioned representation of accepted analytical results for a specific
question and audience. Examples: an order review queue, seller scorecard, category
complaint chart, feature workbook, executive briefing or evidence-linked alert.
A prose paragraph is not the only artifact and a chat message is not the data store.

Artifact contract:
- Audience, owner and intended decision.
- Grain/entity scope, period and timezone.
- Metrics/target definitions and units.
- Source/data/model/policy versions and exact result references.
- Data watermark, generated_at, completeness and expiry/freshness status.
- Actuals versus predictions versus hypotheses clearly labeled.
- Deltas against a named comparable prior snapshot/baseline.
- Evidence links, uncertainty/coverage and known limitations.
- Suggested next step and optional review action; action is not execution authority.
- Access scope, publication state, content hash and supersession history.

Refresh a current-view pointer only after the new version passes validation.
Retain old versions for comparison/retraction. Failed refresh leaves the last good
artifact visible with an explicit stale/failed-refresh indicator, not a fresh date.

2. STAKEHOLDER MATRIX (PROPOSED)

Operations reviewer
  Decision: which open orders deserve attention now?
  Artifact: ranked review queue and individual evidence card.
  Updates: on new eligible predictions, deadline crossings or correction events.
  Escalation: approved risk/deadline/impact rule, or confirmed critical exception.
  Caveat: prediction score freshness and task horizon must match the decision.

Seller/account manager
  Decision: which seller performance changes need investigation?
  Artifact: seller scorecard, trend chart, eligible review excerpts.
  Updates: periodic digest plus a material deterioration case.
  Caveat: restrict attribution where multi-seller order reviews are ambiguous;
  show denominators and excluded cases rather than accusing every seller.

Product/category manager
  Decision: which experience themes are recurring or newly emerging?
  Artifact: theme trends, representative passages, coverage and topic investigation.
  Updates: daily/weekly summary or a corroborated severe-theme exception.
  Caveat: a customer report of a defect is not verified product causality/safety.

Analyst/model owner
  Decision: whether evidence, features and predictors remain trustworthy.
  Artifact: lineage workbook, coverage drift, calibration, error and cost report.
  Updates: dataset/model releases, matured outcomes, evaluation failures.
  Escalation: broken data contracts, loss of freshness or validated model degradation.

Executive
  Decision: where attention/resources are needed across the portfolio.
  Artifact: concise actual-performance briefing, separately labeled forecast,
  top material changes, coverage and links to underlying cases.
  Updates: scheduled digest; immediate interruption only under a separate approved
  aggregate/material-impact policy, not for every individual operational alert.

Native Excel/Word/Slack/Teams integrations remain future adapters. Local HTML,
CSV/XLSX, JSON results and an in-app inbox can demonstrate the behavior first.
Specific file renderers and UI designs are not selected by this document.

3. THREE CLOCKS PLUS ON-DEMAND WORK

A. Event-driven
New source batch, accepted observation, corrected fact, matured outcome, new
prediction or review resolution invalidates only dependent results. Coalesce
related changes for a bounded period; several messages about one order need not
cause several complete rebuilds. Preserve per-entity ordering where required.

B. Scheduled
A digest has its own cadence (e.g. business morning); aggregation/evaluation jobs
may have another cadence. These times are examples, not chosen requirements.
Store schedule timezone, DST behavior and an idempotent scheduled occurrence ID.
A delayed/missed tick follows an explicit catch-up policy; do not blast recipients
with every missed digest after downtime. Recheck current permissions before send.

C. Deadline/time-driven
Some things become important without any new data: a due date approaches, a source
becomes stale, an acknowledgment timer expires. Persist next_evaluation_at and
response deadlines; a durable scheduler wakes the job. Do not occupy a worker in
an infinite sleep loop waiting for the clock.

D. On demand
A stakeholder requests a drill-down or revised view. Reuse accepted snapshots if
appropriate; otherwise request a bounded refresh and show its status. User queries
do not silently change scheduled policy or permit access to broader source data.

Historical replay needs two explicitly different clocks:
- simulation business time, driving Olist event availability and example deadlines;
- wall-clock time, driving worker execution, retries and resource budgets.
No replayed 2018 deadline is a real overdue customer action today. Every replay
artifact and simulated notification must be visibly marked as such.

4. WHAT 'CRITICAL NODES' MEANS IN THIS DESIGN

Two separate meanings:
1. Workflow decision points, implemented as LangGraph nodes.
2. Business attention points: order, seller, category, model or source condition
   whose change may deserve investigation or escalation.

The agent can identify candidate business attention points inside approved scopes.
It does not have authority to add executable graph nodes, invent notification
recipients or rewrite production thresholds simply because source text suggests it.

Logical workflow:
  event/schedule/deadline
    -> resolve affected entities and input manifest
    -> refresh or reuse accepted results
    -> check freshness, coverage, integrity and access
    -> detect rule conditions / statistical changes / semantic candidates
    -> optional bounded investigation
    -> evaluate versioned policy
    -> record immutable decision and update episode
       -> no material change: retain routine result
       -> useful non-urgent change: include in digest
       -> missing/uncertain evidence: local review/question case
       -> approved critical condition: escalation case
    -> render deterministic figures/table + grounded narrative
    -> validate artifact
    -> local publish or explicitly authorized delivery
    -> await response/deadline without holding a worker.

A deterministic critical rule should not wait for an LLM to write a summary.
Publish a minimal evidence card from code; attach explanation later if it passes
validation. If the model/provider is unavailable, independently verified rules
remain evaluable. Do not fabricate a model classification as a fallback.

5. THREE DETECTION ROUTES

5.1 Explicit policy rule
Example: a verified deadline has passed and the event/status policy says fulfillment
is still unresolved. Code evaluates dates/status; Jev is not needed for arithmetic.
The exact outcome/cancellation/source-coverage semantics must be agreed first.

5.2 Statistical or model-derived change
Example: a comparable seller metric rises above a baseline by an approved amount,
with sufficient eligible cases, coverage and persistence. Define both minimum
absolute and relative effects where useful. Sampling error, repeated scanning and
multiple comparisons can create false alarms; use validated control limits/FDR or
review screening, and measure false-alert rates rather than calling every spike real.
Predicted probability thresholds require a calibrated/validated task and current
feature/model contract. Model-version changes are annotated/rebaselined, not
silently presented as a business deterioration.

5.3 Agent-proposed exception
Jev or an investigative agent identifies a theme, conflict or unusual combination
not captured by a simple rule. It emits a typed candidate with:
  entity/scope, concern category, evidence IDs, novelty, suggested severity,
  uncertainty, proposed next step and the policy provision it claims applies to.
Code checks provenance, permissions, budget, eligibility and approved routing.
Unless a preapproved semantic rule explicitly authorizes escalation, a novel
candidate goes to human triage, not an external alarm or automatic business action.

Business risk and evidence uncertainty are different axes:
- High supported impact + adequate evidence -> eligible for approved escalation.
- High potential impact + uncertain evidence -> urgent verification when policy allows.
- Low impact + adequate evidence -> ordinary digest or no notification.
- Low impact + weak evidence -> bounded investigation or abstention.
Weak evidence is not automatically low business risk. Conversely a confident
classifier is not proof of high business impact. Jev confidence is not downstream
outcome probability or authorization.

6. A VERSIONED ESCALATION POLICY

Each policy needs at least:
- ID/version, owner, approval state, effective time and allowed scope.
- Trigger events/cadence, event-time watermark and allowed lateness.
- Metric/target/feature/model eligibility and comparison baseline.
- Freshness, source coverage, sample-size and provenance requirements.
- Enter/escalate/clear conditions, persistence and hysteresis.
- Severity mapping and uncertainty handling.
- Recipient role resolution and channel allowlist.
- Quiet hours, digest/coalescing windows, repeat/reminder limits.
- Deduplication key and material-change definition.
- Acknowledgment/resolution deadlines and approved fallback owner.
- Allowed action: local display, notify, ask, request approval; execution of business
  changes is a distinct authority, not implied by escalation.
- Cost/latency budgets, audit requirements, review date and expiry.

Policy definitions are data interpreted by deterministic code, not arbitrary
agent-generated Python. The agent can propose a new version with examples and
estimated effects; changes require review, replay evaluation and explicit activation.
Record the version on every decision. Never retroactively reinterpret a past alert
under a new policy without an explicit reevaluation record.

7. ILLUSTRATIVE ROLLING-RISK POLICY — NOT APPROVED

Policy: rolling_order_risk_demo_v1
Scope: eligible open orders in a LOCAL historical replay.
Task dependency: a separately evaluated rolling (not approval-only) predictor.
Trigger: accepted score/deadline/status event or scheduled reevaluation.
Evidence gates:
  approved predictor and feature contract; required source watermark current;
  no missing required features or unresolved ownership/status conflict.
Example enter condition:
  p_late >= 0.75 AND time_to_deadline in (0,24] hours,
  sustained for 2 complete evaluations at least 15 simulated minutes apart.
Example clear condition:
  p_late <= 0.55 for 2 complete evaluations,
  OR a verified final status satisfying the approved close policy.
Routing: local Operations review inbox; no external channel.
Acknowledgment example: 2 simulated business hours.
No acknowledgment: create a local lead-review event under the same episode;
  notify the approved fallback only if such a recipient/action has been authorized.
Repeats: at most one unchanged reminder per 4 simulated hours, maximum 2 reminders;
  then retain as unresolved and route through the approved timeout disposition.
Material severity increase or independently verified critical breach:
  bypass ordinary cooldown once per new severity transition, preserving deduplication.

All numbers above are placeholders for replay testing, not business defaults.
A p=0.75 threshold is not warranted before calibration/capacity assessment.
Repeated duplicate runs are not two independent qualifying evaluations.
No new data can still justify a deadline reevaluation, but does not create invented
new evidence. Stale/incomplete data yields unknown and a data-health case, not
clear/on-time. Budget exhaustion also does not mean the business condition cleared.

A parallel data-health policy is evaluated independently:
if required input freshness/integrity fails, hold affected new predictions and
notify the appropriate data owner under that policy; existing risk episodes become
unknown/stale, not automatically resolved. Data-health incidents have their own
severities and do not share every minimum-sample gate with statistical detection.

Hysteresis (different enter and clear thresholds) reduces flapping; sustained
conditions reduce transient noise. Both can delay detection, so immediate critical
conditions and urgent verification must have explicit approved exceptions.

8. EPISODES, ACTIONS AND ACKNOWLEDGMENT

An episode groups one continuing concern rather than creating a new incident for
every refreshed score. Suggested identity:
  access_scope + policy_family + entity_or_group + concern_type.
A policy-version transition has an explicit migrate/rebaseline/close policy; it
must not open duplicate alerts merely because its version string changed.

Maintain two related but separate state dimensions:
- Condition: normal, watch, active, unknown, cleared.
- Response: unassigned, assigned, acknowledged, investigating, resolved, dismissed.
Severity, suppression window and next_evaluation_at are separate fields.
Acknowledgment does not imply the underlying condition improved. Dismissal needs
a reason and duration; it cannot erase evidence or suppress unrelated critical facts.
A delivery receipt is not human acknowledgment. Quiet-hours suppression is not
resolution. Explicitly record reopen events if a cleared concern returns.

Concurrent workers update an episode using transactions/locking or optimistic
version checks. Persist entry time, qualifying evaluations, last material change,
last notification, acknowledgment deadline and escalation level. No essential state
lives only in an in-memory graph execution or Redis event stream.

9. ADDITIONS TO THE RELATIONAL DATA MODEL

artifact_definitions
  definition_id/version, role, question, grain, metric_refs, view/template,
  refresh_triggers, timezone, freshness/coverage_rules, access_scope, approval.

artifact_runs and artifact_versions
  build attempt and immutable published version, accepted result IDs,
  data_watermark, generated_at, renderer version, validation, URI/hash, supersedes.
  These refine the earlier artifacts contract rather than duplicate it; final
  schema naming is an implementation decision.

escalation_policies
  policy_id/version, owner, structured conditions, gates, routing, response timers,
  severity/clearing rules, approval/effective interval and definition hash.

policy_evaluations
  evaluation_id, policy_version, entity_scope, evaluation_at, trigger_id,
  input/result/prediction refs, observed operands, pass/fail/unknown gates,
  outcome (none/digest/review/escalate/clear/blocked), reason_codes,
  agent_candidate_ref nullable, cost, episode_ref.

attention_episodes
  episode_id, logical_concern_key, condition_state, response_state, severity,
  owner_role, current_policy_version, first_seen_at, last_evaluated_at,
  last_material_change_at, next_evaluation_at, acknowledgment_due_at,
  optimistic_version, current_evidence_manifest_ref.

episode_events
  immutable transitions, evidence links, user acknowledgments, assignments,
  questions/answers, suppression decisions, corrections, retractions and resolution.

notification_outbox / delivery_attempts
  durable logical delivery identity, episode/digest/artifact version,
  resolved authorized recipient/channel, status, attempt sequence,
  provider receipt, sent_at and uncertain-outcome/reconciliation status.

replay_scenarios (local evidence fixtures, table or versioned files)
  simulated clock, input event manifest, approved demo policy version,
  expected decisions/artifacts, actual trace and evidence disposition.

Foreign keys, access scope and retention must connect artifacts and decisions back
to exact accepted evidence. Recipient scopes can be narrower than the data analyst's
scope; redact or regenerate results before delivery. Do not leak restricted data
through a title, aggregate, chart, explanation or notification preview.

10. ARTIFACT GENERATION AND DELIVERY SAFETY

Build result bundle with SQL/approved analytic tools. Validate counts, units,
filters, joins, dates, denominators and actual-versus-predicted labels. A generative
presenter can select a template and write grounded explanations. It cannot invent
numbers, source links, causal explanations, confidence intervals or actions taken.
Use deterministic chart rendering from structured specifications where possible.
If prose fails checks or the provider is down, retain a factual table/card fallback.

Publisher transaction records accepted artifact version and logical outbox event.
Delivery worker rechecks authorization and episode/artifact freshness, then sends
only to approved recipients. For stale queued alerts, suppress or replace according
to policy; preserve the audit trail. Use provider idempotency where available.
A crash after sending but before recording the receipt creates an uncertain outcome;
reconcile rather than promise exactly-once notification. Retry budgets and duplicate
protections are needed even if Aegra resumes perfectly.

Change comparison should use normalized result content, not a newly worded LLM
summary: different wording of identical facts is not a material business update.
Corrections may require an explicit retraction/replacement notification, not silent
editing of the old briefing. A later permission revocation can block new deliveries
but cannot guarantee deletion of a message already delivered to an external system.

11. LOCAL DEMONSTRATION STORYBOARD

Use deterministic fixtures and simulated business time before real model calls.
Display side by side: event timeline, pipeline progress, stakeholder artifacts,
policy operands/gates, episode state, notifications and evidence drill-down.

Example progression (all simulated):
08:00: scheduled digest publishes accepted baseline; no critical case.
09:00: new evidence produces a changed analytical result; dashboard refreshes.
09:15: second qualifying evaluation enters active risk; Operations card appears.
09:20: replay duplicate event; no duplicate episode or delivery.
09:30: reviewer acknowledges; condition remains active, response is acknowledged.
10:00: required source becomes stale; score refresh blocks, card shows unknown;
       data-owner case appears; business episode is not declared cleared.
10:30: source recovers and new accepted result increases severity; an approved
       severity transition can update the case despite ordinary reminder cooldown.
11:00: valid final outcome arrives; close policy records resolution and history.
12:00: digest includes the resolved episode without pretending a customer was contacted.

Separate cases demonstrate no acknowledgment, quiet hours, provider failure,
agent-discovered ambiguity, rejected policy proposal, source correction and worker
restart. Not every case must appear in the same compressed demonstration.

12. REQUIRED EVIDENCE BEFORE CLAIMING DYNAMIC INTELLIGENCE

- Artifact values reconcile to exact query results and immutable manifests.
- Future/late evidence cannot leak into an earlier replayed prediction or digest.
- Approval-only versus rolling prediction contracts cannot be mixed silently.
- Threshold boundary, persistence, hysteresis and deadline behavior is deterministic.
- Stale/unknown does not equal false/clear; data-health rules still run.
- Duplicate input, concurrent evaluation and resume do not duplicate logical episodes.
- Cooldown suppresses repeats but approved severity transitions still surface once.
- Quiet hours, time zones/DST and missed schedules have tested behavior.
- Acknowledgment, delivery and resolution remain distinguishable.
- Unapproved agent severity/recipient/policy changes cannot execute external actions.
- A deterministic critical card is available without generative-model success.
- Correction/retraction and previously queued stale-alert handling work as specified.
- End-to-end restart and uncertain-send handling retain an auditable disposition.
- Rendered stakeholder views expose freshness, evidence and labels clearly.
- Measure precision/usefulness of alerts, missed critical cases, response effort,
  reviewer load, latency/lead time and per-update cost; do not use volume of alerts
  as a success metric. Detection delay and notification fatigue are trade-offs.

Evidence must identify candidate commit, fixture/data, model and policy versions.
Required evidence ends passed/failed/blocked; none has been executed for this design.

13. FIRST HUMAN DECISION AND FOLLOW-ON WORK

First decision: for the MVP, may the agent autonomously create/update LOCAL review
cases under approved policies, while new policies/recipients and all outward
notifications/actions require approval? This is a proposed authority boundary,
not an assumed permission.

After that decision, define one stakeholder workflow at a time: owner and intended
action, trusted evidence, artifact, enter/clear rules, timing, review capacity and
success measures. Then evaluate candidate thresholds with historical replay and
human review. Do not implement a generic policy language or many integrations
before demonstrating one complete observe -> explain -> review -> resolve loop.

This document is our design synthesis, not a claim that Aegra or LangGraph ships
these domain-specific policies. Runtime capabilities and their primary sources are
recorded in open-source-runtime-study.txt and langchain-engineering-study.txt.
